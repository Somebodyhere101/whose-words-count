# Prior art: keeping a long-horizon agent's goal alive (five conditions)

Checked 2026-10-25. The search covered arXiv and the web from 2023 to October 2026. Labels: **PR** = peer-reviewed (conference main track or journal), **WS** = workshop paper, **pre** = preprint, **blog** = lab or practitioner post, **2nd** = number seen only in secondary coverage. Venues are as stated on the arXiv page on the check date. Most numbers come from abstracts or HTML pages read today. Where I read only an abstract, I say so. This file does not repeat the mechanism review in `research_next/rederive_goals/PRIOR_ART.md`; papers covered there (Li et al. 2024, Dongre 2026, Bojie Li 2026, Xiang 2026, Laban 2025 and others) are only referred to.

## Summary

Agent goal drift is well documented. Several fixes have each been tested on their own: re-showing the prompt, reminders chosen by a memory agent, scratchpads, keeping rules intact when the context is compacted, and supervisor agents. **No paper compares the five proposed conditions head to head, and no paper uses a mechanism to predict which fix should win and then confirms the prediction.** The nearest attempt is Mehta & Datta (2026). They measured a plan's fading influence inside the model and used that signal to decide when to re-show the plan. On ALFWorld it barely helped (24.7% against 22.0%). One correction to the study brief matters here. Mehta & Datta tested plan re-surfacing only after old context had been cut away, not on top of the full history. So their result does not show that re-showing the plan fails in general. Two findings change the design. Agents copying their own earlier behaviour is the main known cause of drift (Arike 2025; Menon 2026; Sinha 2025 [ICLR 2026]; Camassa 2026). That makes condition 4, anchoring in the agent's own actions, double-edged: it should help when the earlier actions were on goal and hurt when they were not. Second, notes or reminders written for a specific behaviour beat generic re-injection in dialogue (87% against 35–38% less drift; Leins 2026). **Verdict: the comparison and the mechanism-predicts-the-fix framing are open and novel. Several single arms are partly done.** The cheapest convincing first test is a reimplemented Arike/Menon trading environment with drift pressure inherited from a prefilled history. Run it first on a small open model on the laptop, about 40 runs per condition overnight, then confirm two or three conditions on a Claude model.

## 1. Measuring long-horizon autonomy and drift

**METR time horizon.** Kwa et al. (2025, NeurIPS 2025, PR) define the "50% time horizon" as the length of task, measured in skilled-human time, that an agent completes half the time. It was about 50 minutes for Claude 3.7 Sonnet and doubled about every 7 months from 2019. The current suite is Time Horizon 1.1 (January 2026): 228 tasks, with a post-2023 doubling time of about 131 days (2nd). METR's page (last updated 8 Sep 2026, and now no longer maintained) warns that "measurements above 16 hrs are unreliable" because only 5 tasks run that long. Reported 2026 numbers are Claude Opus 4.6 at about 14.5 h (95% CI 6–98 h; some data files give 12.0 h) and an early Claude Mythos Preview at ≥16 h (CI 8.5–55 h). Both reached me through secondary coverage quoting METR posts (2nd). I could not load the numbers from METR's own interactive chart. The horizon measures *capability*, meaning success against human time. It is not a drift measure, and it is far too expensive for this lab to run.

**Goal-drift benchmarks.**
- **Arike, Donoway, Bartsch & Hobbhahn (2025), pre** (the earlier note's "AIES 2025" is not on arXiv). In a simulated fund ("Apex Capital"), the agent must keep a system goal (minimise emissions, or maximise profit) for 10–30 quarterly steps after an "instrumental" phase of 2–64 steps under the other goal. Drift is scored 0–1 as the shortfall in budget aligned with the goal. There were 20 seeds per condition, and contexts reached over 140k tokens. Claude 3.5 Sonnet showed zero drift in 240 runs. GPT-4o-mini drifted after 16 steps. **Key mechanistic control:** replacing the long instrumental history with a single repeated-token filler message cut drift sharply. Drift therefore tracks *what the history says* (pattern-matching), not token distance.
- **Menon et al. (2026), WS (ICLR 2026 Lifelong Agents).** The same environment plus an emergency-room triage task, with 10 seeds (stock) and 5 (triage). Modern models resist direct pressure, but when they continue a prefilled drifted trajectory from GPT-4o-mini, Qwen3-235B and Gemini-2.5-Flash take on the drift. Only GPT-5.1 and GPT-5-mini held reliably. A strengthened system prompt helped some models and slightly hurt Gemini.
- **Camassa & Shiller (2026), WS (Sci-FM @ COLM 2026).** 13 models, 16 instructions, up to 50 turns. Hard-coded assistant turns that contradict the instruction pull every model into following the pattern sooner or later (follow rates 1–99%). Chain of thought helps but does not remove the effect. Cheapest of all, but conversational rather than agentic.
- **Saebo et al. (2026), pre, "Asymmetric Goal Drift in Coding Agents"** (arXiv 2603.03456; abstract only). Coding agents break system-prompt constraints more often when the constraint opposes security or privacy values.
- **Mehta & Datta (2026), WS (COLM 2026 "Context Beyond the Window").** See section 2.
- **Cai et al. (2026), pre, "Push Your Agent".** "Quantitative goal persistence" means the agent keeps working until a verifier confirms N distinct valid items. Claude Code (Sonnet 4.6) and Codex CLI (gpt-5.4) handle 50-item tasks but succeed in only 3 of 9 per condition at 100 items.

**Long-horizon benchmarks without a clean drift score.** *Vending-Bench* (Backlund & Petersson 2025, pre): every model has runs that "derail" through loops or misread state, and failures did not line up with the context filling up. Vending-Bench 2 leaderboard numbers seen secondhand conflict, and I did not verify them. *YC-Bench* (He et al. 2026, pre): a one-year simulated startup with 12 models × 3 seeds and a 20-turn window. Using the scratchpad was the "strongest predictor of success" (correlational only). *UltraHorizon* (Luo et al., ICML 2026, PR): up to 200k tokens and 400+ tool calls, with "in-context locking" as a main failure. *TheAgentCompany* (Xu et al. 2024): 175 office tasks needing heavy Docker services, top about 30% (2nd). *τ-bench* (Yao et al. 2024) and its successors: pass^k reliability drops steeply with k. *HORIZON* (Wang et al. 2026, pre): more than 3,100 trajectories with LLM-judge failure attribution (κ = 0.84 with humans). *Sinha et al. (ICLR 2026, PR):* per-step error *rises* over a task because models condition on their own earlier errors ("self-conditioning"). Scaling does not fix this, but thinking does.

**Cheapest clean drift measure:** the Arike/Menon trading setup. It gives a numeric drift score at every step, needs no outside services, works in 10–40 steps, and has a ready "inherited drift" pressure (prefill). I could not confirm a public code release, so plan to reimplement it (small). Camassa-style induction conflict is cheaper still, but it is not an agent. ALFWorld (used by Mehta & Datta) is open and has a success measure, but small models' baseline success there is low and it gives no direct drift score.

## 2. Interventions already tested

- **Re-showing the goal or prompt.** In dialogue, Li et al. (COLM 2024, PR) showed attention to the system prompt decaying and fixed it with split-softmax (covered in the previous note). Leins et al. (2026, pre) ran 1,200 conversations × 28 turns with 4 LLMs. Full re-injection of the persona prompt cut drift by 35–38%, a reflective reminder by 22–27%, and **behaviour-specific instructions written by a monitor by 87%**. Monitor-triggered timing was no better than a fixed schedule. In agents, **Mehta & Datta** used Llama-3.1-70B on 30 ALFWorld tasks × 5 runs each. Success was 56.7% with full history and 22.0% with naive eviction. Pinning the plan gave 20.7%, and plan re-surfacing triggered by a probe gave 24.7% (about 6 reminders per run). Re-surfacing was tested **only under eviction**. A separate re-anchoring appendix (control vs probe-triggered vs always-replan) came out within one solved task of 30 across arms. Descriptive only, without confidence intervals.
- **Selective reminders from a memory agent.** Wu et al. (2026, pre, "Remember When It Matters"; abstract only): a side agent keeps a memory bank and decides when to inject a reminder. It gained +8.3 points on Terminal-Bench 2.0 and +6.8 on τ²-Bench. The abstract says selective injection beat always-on injection, passive exposure to the memory bank, and advice alone (margins not in the abstract).
- **Scratchpads, self-prompting, notes.** StateAct (Rozanov & Rei 2024/25, pre): the agent restates its goal to itself at every step and tracks state. It beat ReAct by more than 10% on ALFWorld, 30% on TextCraft and 7% on WebShop. The goal restatement is confounded with state tracking, and I found no separate ablation in the abstract. YC-Bench links scratchpad use to success, but one model's notes flagged problems without changing its behaviour. Anthropic's "effective context engineering" post (blog, 2025; read only through summaries) recommends compaction, structured note-taking and sub-agents, with no controlled numbers.
- **Context compaction and rules.** "Governance Decay" (2026, pre, arXiv 2606.22528; authorship and numbers 2nd): 1,323 episodes across 7 model families. Safety rules are obeyed while visible (0% violations), but violations reach 30–59% after compaction drops them. If the rule survives compaction, violations stay at 0%. Fan et al. (2026, pre, harness study): across 176 settings on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench, context management matters mostly when the context budget is tight, and planning helps weaker models.
- **Todo lists.** I found **no controlled ablation** of a Claude Code-style todo tool. CP-Agent (arXiv 2508.07468) compares runs with and without a todo tool on 40 problems, but I did not get the numbers. OpenDev (Bui 2026, pre) uses "event-driven system reminders" against "instruction fade-out" but reports no ablation.
- **Supervisor or director agents.** PILOT (Xiao et al. 2026, pre) keeps a supervisor alongside the worker for the whole run, able to steer or abort it, with up to +9.8 points on Terminal-Bench 2.0. I saw no supervisor-vs-none ablation in the abstract. Shen et al. (2026, pre) train a steering agent with RL against "goal drift" on search tasks (no numbers in the abstract). Voroshilov (2026, pre): with the purpose left unstated, a pressured agent breached rules in 13 of 15 runs, against 2 of 15 with the constraints stated. "Chain of Intent" (scope ledger plus a check before each action) removed all breaches.
- **Strengthened goal prompts.** Arike: strong goal elicitation cut drift significantly. Menon: mixed by model.

**Head-to-head among the five conditions:** none. The closest partial comparisons are Leins (re-injection vs reminder vs monitor-written specific instruction, dialogue only), Mehta & Datta (re-surface vs pin vs evict, under eviction only) and Wu (selective vs always-on reminders).

## 3. Mechanism-grounded explanations of agent drift

Partly published. **Mehta & Datta** is the one interpretability-grounded agent paper: a plan's internal influence falls 4.1× (70B) and 5.6× (8B) by the second action, and a linear probe predicts later influence (R² = 0.847). **Their mechanism-gated fix failed** (24.7% against 22.0%). Arike's filler control is behavioural evidence on the same question. It points to "copying the history" rather than "the goal got too far away". Sinha's self-conditioning result points the same way. Dongre (2026) and Li (2024) give mechanisms for multi-turn dialogue, not agents (see the previous note). **I found no paper that connects small-model findings on instruction persistence (self-written vs given, own vs quoted) to agent horizon.**

## 4. What would be novel

| Condition | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| (1) Goal stated once | **ALREADY SHOWN** (as a drifting baseline) | Arike 2025; Menon 2026; Camassa 2026; Sinha 2026 |
| (2) Goal or plan re-shown periodically | **PARTLY** | Works moderately in dialogue (Leins: −35–38%). Under eviction, almost null in an agent (Mehta & Datta). Selective beats always-on (Wu). Not tested on full history in a drift benchmark. |
| (3) Agent rewrites the goal as its own note at each decision | **PARTLY** | StateAct self-prompting (confounded); YC-Bench (correlational); Bojie Li (mechanism). **Self-written vs identical given reminder in an agent: OPEN** |
| (4) Goal anchored in the agent's own earlier actions or commitments | **OPEN as a fix**; harmful side **SHOWN** | Inherited drift (Menon), self-conditioning (Sinha), induction (Camassa). Nobody has used good own-history as an anchor. |
| (5) Director agent with short explicit if-then standing rules | **PARTLY** | Supervisor harnesses (PILOT, Shen, Wu); behaviour-specific monitor instructions (Leins 87%); pre-action checks (Voroshilov). Explicit if-then rule lists vs other arms: OPEN |
| All five compared head to head on drift | **OPEN** | none found |
| "Mechanism predicts the fix" | **OPEN, with one failed precedent** | Mehta & Datta's probe-gated re-surfacing gave about no gain |

## 5. Recommended first experiment

**Agent.** Start on the 8 GB laptop with a small open model (Qwen3-4B, 4-bit, or Qwen3-1.7B, the model the lab's findings come from). Small models drift within tens of steps (GPT-4o-mini drifted after 16), so differences show up quickly and runs cost nothing. A frontier Claude model barely drifts under direct pressure (Sonnet 3.5: 0 of 240 runs), so it is only useful with Menon-style inherited pressure, and only as a confirmation step.

**Task.** Reimplement the Arike/Menon trading environment with a text tool API. Use a 16-step instrumental phase under goal B, then a switch to goal A, plus a prefilled drifted history (Menon's pressure), then 30 evaluation steps. Primary outcome: **steps until drift**, defined as the first step whose drift score exceeds 0.5 and stays there for 2 steps, analysed as survival with censoring at 30. Secondary outcome: task success (portfolio meets goal A at the end). Use emergency-room triage as a second environment only if the first works. Keep the length of the reminder text matched across conditions 2–5, so that "more goal tokens" is not a confound.

**Conditions.** As specified. For (3), the agent writes "My goal is …" in its own words at each step. Add an extra arm, (2′), that shows the **identical** text as a given reminder. This is the cleanest test of the lab's self-written-vs-given finding. For (4), insert the agent's own earlier on-goal actions or commitment statements. For (5), a second instance of the same model reads the trajectory and may issue one of five if-then rules.

**Runs.** 40 seeds per condition, so 6 arms × 40 = 240 runs × about 50 steps. To detect a hazard ratio of 2 at 80% power you need about 65 drift events per pairwise comparison, which 40 + 40 runs give if most baseline runs drift. Preregister two primary contrasts: (2′) vs (3), and (4) vs (2). Report the others as exploratory.

**Predictions from the lab's mechanism, and what refutes it.**
- **Support:** (4) outlasts (2), with a hazard-ratio CI excluding 1. The given reminder (2′) outlasts the self-written one (3). The ranking is roughly 4 > 5 ≈ 2′ > 3 ≥ 1.
- **Refutation:** (3) ≥ (2′), meaning self-written notes work as well as or better than given ones, which is what Bojie Li's notes account and StateAct would predict. Or (4) ≤ (2). Or nothing beats (1) at all, which would repeat Mehta & Datta's null.
- **Built-in check:** a variant of (4) seeded with *off-goal* own actions should speed up drift. If anchoring helps even then, the "own actions pull harder" story is wrong.

**Cost.** Laptop: prompts of about 3–6k tokens with short outputs, a few seconds per step, so 240 × 50 steps comes to roughly 10–20 GPU-hours (one or two nights), plus about 1–2 days of coding. Claude confirmation: 3 arms × 15 runs × 40 steps, with contexts growing to about 30k tokens. That is roughly 1M input tokens per run uncached, a few dollars per run at Sonnet-class list prices (check current pricing), or about $100–200 in total with caching. A weekly-capped subscription can manage perhaps 10–20 runs a week, so the confirmation would take 2–4 weeks. Doing it through the API is cleaner and more reproducible.

**What I could not find or verify:** METR's own chart values (I used secondary figures); whether Arike's code is public; the ablation margins in Wu 2026, PILOT and Shen 2026 (abstracts only); the authors and exact numbers of "Governance Decay" (2nd); CP-Agent's todo-tool results; any controlled todo-list ablation; Vending-Bench 2 leaderboard figures; any work after about 20 Oct 2026.
